# iPod Classic Modding Guide

An independent, practical guide to restoring and modifying repairable iPods. It is written for people who want a reliable player—not just an impressive parts list.

> **Work in progress.** Every procedure should be tested on the stated model before it is presented as complete. Do not rely on placeholder links, unverified capacity claims, or a parts list alone.

## How to use this guide

1. **Identify before buying.** “Classic,” “5th gen,” “6th gen,” and “7th gen” are not interchangeable. Confirm the model, original capacity, case thickness, and firmware before selecting parts.
2. **Choose a build, then check fitment.** Storage adapter, battery size, and thin/thick rear case are a single fitment decision.
3. **Read the whole procedure once.** Set out tools, back up the iPod, and open every required reference before prying the case.
4. **Test while it is open.** Restore, charge, play audio, and verify controls before snapping the metal case shut.

## Safety baseline

- Back up the iPod before any hardware work.
- Fully discharge it and stop if the lithium-ion battery is swollen, hot, torn, or smells unusual. Do not puncture, bend, or reuse a damaged cell.
- The 6th/7th-generation metal Classic is difficult to open and can be cosmetically damaged. Work slowly, protect the display, and expect clips to need attention.
- Bluetooth, USB-C data, power-bank-cell conversions, and board-level repairs are advanced work. They must have their own model-specific guide, clear soldering requirements, and a recovery plan before being recommended here.

Hardware guides need traceable sources. Vendor documentation is used for compatibility and installation details; iFixit is used for disassembly safety and photos; official software documentation is preferred for restoration and alternative firmware. Community reports are useful leads, but are labeled as such until independently reproduced. See [sources and buying guidance](docs/General/sourcing-parts.md).
