# Tools and workspace

Buy tools for the procedure in front of you. A storage-only upgrade does not require every tool used for board-level or cosmetic work.

## Essential for a metal Classic

| Tool | Why it matters | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plastic opening tools and thin case-opening tools | Start and hold the seam open | Expect wear; metal Classics are difficult to open. |
| Fine tweezers or nylon spudger | Operate delicate connector latches | Use the smallest force possible. |
| Kapton tape | Insulate and restrain parts | Never use it to force an incompatible fit. |
| Microfiber cloth and display protection | Avoid cosmetic damage | Protect the display before opening. |
| Small parts tray | Prevent lost screws and brackets | Label parts by removal order. |
| Good light and a clean, non-conductive surface | See latches and avoid contamination | An anti-static mat is helpful, but careful handling matters more than a cluttered bench. |

## Only when the procedure calls for them

- Precision drivers for internal screws on the relevant model.
- Isopropyl alcohol for adhesive residue; keep it away from displays and allow it to evaporate fully.
- Replacement rear case, if the planned battery/adapter combination requires additional depth.
- Soldering equipment, magnification, fume extraction, and a multimeter for an explicitly documented board-level modification.

## Before you open the case

- Back up the iPod and charge/discharge it to a safe level according to the repair reference.
- Photograph the exterior and record model, serial, capacity, and existing faults.
- Put the hold switch in the locked position if the model-specific procedure says to.
- Stop and seek a battery-specific repair/disposal route if the cell is swollen or damaged.

> **Read first:** iFixit’s [iPod Classic battery procedure](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/iPod+Classic+Battery+Replacement/561) describes the difficulty and battery precautions for the metal Classic. Follow its model-specific photos alongside this guide when opening a case.
