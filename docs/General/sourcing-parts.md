# Parts, sourcing, and evidence

The right part is one that fits the exact iPod and can be traced back to a credible listing—not simply the cheapest listing with the right keywords.

## Buy in this order

1. Identify the iPod and rear-case thickness.
2. Pick the storage adapter and media type.
3. Confirm the adapter/battery/rear-case fit as one combination.
4. Buy a reputable battery and only then consider cosmetic or connectivity parts.

## Starting sources

| Need | First reference | What to confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Storage adapter and media preparation | [iFlash preparation documentation](https://www.iflash.xyz/prepare-sdxc-exfat-for-use-with-the-ipod/) | Exact iPod/firmware, adapter revision, card capacity, and current preparation notes. |
| Opening, connectors, and repair photos | [iFixit iPod Classic repair hub](https://www.ifixit.com/Device/iPod_Classic) | Guide matches your model and the part you intend to disturb. |
| iPod identification | [Apple iPod identification](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/103823) | Model and original capacity, not colour alone. |
| Alternative firmware | [Rockbox iPod Classic manual](https://download.rockbox.org/manual/rockbox-ipod6g/rockbox-build.html) | Supported target, current installation steps, and uninstall/recovery instructions. |

Specialist retailers and marketplace sellers may be excellent sources, but link to the exact product page only after confirming the listing, revision, shipping region, and warranty. Prices are volatile; use “checked on” dates instead of evergreen dollar amounts.

## Listing checklist

- Does the listing name the correct model and thin/thick case compatibility?
- Is the battery physically sized for the planned build, rather than only advertised by mAh?
- Is the storage media from a known retailer with a return path?
- Is there a current installation guide, documentation, or credible support channel?
- Can the item be returned if it fails the open-case test?

## Claims policy

Do not publish “works with,” capacity ceilings, runtimes, charge times, heat figures, or SD-brand exclusions as universal facts without an exact model, adapter, media, firmware, method, date, and source. A community report is valuable evidence, but label it as one. A tested configuration should include a build list and a short test log.
